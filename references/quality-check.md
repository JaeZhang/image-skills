# Quality check

Score each image out of 100. Recommend regeneration or targeted editing below 85, or whenever a mandatory product lock fails.

## Aesthetic checklist

Treat aesthetics as observable decisions, not a collection of flattering adjectives. Score these questions before delivery:

- Focal point: Can a viewer identify the product within three seconds at thumbnail size? Is there one dominant visual focus?
- Subject scale: Is the product large enough to read while preserving its important silhouette and a deliberate breathing margin?
- Composition balance: Are visual weights stable? Are there awkward tangencies, random spacing, accidental crops, or unsupported props?
- Negative space: Is empty space intentional and placed where it can support product clarity or later copy?
- Color hierarchy: Does the palette use the product color, a restrained neutral background, and no more than one meaningful accent? Does the background support rather than compete?
- Edge separation: Can every important product edge be read against the background, including dark-on-dark and pale-on-white cases?
- Material light: Does light reveal the actual material—fiber direction, glaze gradient, metal edge highlight, leather sheen, or paper surface—without plastic-looking gloss or dead flatness?
- Depth: Is the sharpness hierarchy appropriate to the image role? Are selling points in focus and the background softened only where useful?
- Props and mood: Does every prop explain scale, use, material, or mood? Would removing it make the image clearer?
- Series coherence: When viewed beside the rest of the listing, do aspect ratio, color temperature, background family, margins, and shadow language feel like one visual system?

If two or more answers are uncertain, simplify the image before adding more styling. Fix hierarchy, contrast, spacing, and material light before pursuing a more elaborate mood.

## Scoring

- Product fidelity — 35: silhouette, proportions, color, pattern, components, hardware, and count match the product reference.
- Structural and physical validity — 20: attachments, hands, gravity, support, perspective, reflections, and occlusion are plausible.
- Composition and thumbnail clarity — 15: focal hierarchy, crop, scale, negative space, and edge separation work at small size.
- Material and lighting — 15: material reads correctly; highlights and shadows reveal form without clipping or muddiness.
- Color harmony — 10: palette supports the product, skin and whites remain believable, and subject/background separation is sufficient.
- Cleanliness — 5: no unintended text, watermark, duplicate object, artifact, clutter, or inconsistent panel.

## Mandatory rejection conditions

- wrong product variant, color, pattern, count, or key component;
- added or missing strap, handle, lid, clasp, pocket, trim, prop, or costume accessory;
- garbled or invented label text presented as final copy;
- fused, floating, intersecting, anatomically broken, or physically unsupported elements;
- collage panels that depict different product designs when consistency was required;
- product edge lost against the background or critical selling feature cropped/covered.

## Correction strategy

Prefer a localized edit when composition and most product locks are correct. Regenerate when silhouette, perspective, overall lighting, or multiple locked features are wrong. Preserve accepted regions and state exactly what may change.
