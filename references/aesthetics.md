# Aesthetic direction

Use visual choices that support the product instead of decorating by habit.

## Composition

- Design first for thumbnail legibility: one clear focal point and a readable silhouette.
- Hero image default: centered or subtly off-center, product occupying about 65–78% of the frame, with 12–18% breathing room unless the product needs scale context.
- Use visual balance, not automatic symmetry. Symmetry suits formal, premium, or single-object shots; controlled asymmetry suits lifestyle and variant arrangements.
- Keep props subordinate in size, contrast, saturation, and sharpness. Every prop must clarify scale, use, material, or mood.
- Avoid tangencies: handles, limbs, rims, labels, and props should not awkwardly touch frame edges or each other.
- Preserve believable gravity, support, contact points, perspective, and occlusion.

## Color

- Start from the product's dominant hue and choose a restrained palette: product color, one supporting neutral, and at most one accent.
- Ensure clear luminance separation between the product edge and background. Do not place dark products on equally dark backgrounds or pale products on nearly identical whites without rim separation.
- For saturated products, prefer low-saturation neutrals. For neutral products, use a controlled muted accent only when it strengthens category positioning.
- Keep whites neutral unless a warm lifestyle mood is intentional. Prevent skin, fabric, ceramic, and paper whites from sharing an artificial tint.
- State the intended palette with descriptive names and optional hex targets, but allow photographic variation rather than requiring pixel-exact backgrounds.

## Depth and hierarchy

- Hero/SKU images: crisp product, quiet background, restrained depth.
- Lifestyle images: foreground, subject, and softened background may form three depth layers.
- Detail images: one feature per frame; macro sharpness should fall on the feature, not nearby decoration.
- Use negative space deliberately for later copy. Specify its side and approximate percentage.

## Style discipline

- Use film grain only for editorial or lifestyle scenes, not by default for white-background, label-heavy, size, or material-detail images.
- Avoid combining incompatible aesthetics such as flat shadowless catalog light with dramatic stage light.
- Prefer a single coherent mood: clean-natural, warm-artisanal, dark-luxury, playful-child, or cinematic-costume.
- Avoid generic luxury props, excessive flowers, random books, floating particles, heavy bokeh, and decorative clutter unless the product story calls for them.
