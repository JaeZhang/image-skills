# Product fidelity and lighting rules

Read only the relevant category section. Turn each rule into observable prompt language.

## Bags

Lock silhouette, width-height-depth ratio, handle count and length, opening, seams, pockets, hardware color, closures, straps, print content, and print placement. Require natural fabric tension, believable weight, supported contact, and no hook/handle penetration. Use a 30–45 degree soft key for canvas, corduroy, or knit texture; add restrained fill so deep colors retain detail.

## Cups and ceramics

Lock body profile, rim ellipse, base, handle attachment, lid fit, pattern placement, glaze color, and component count. Prevent warped rims, mismatched lids, liquid unless requested, floating components, or impossible stacking. Use a large side-front soft source to create a readable glaze gradient without clipped reflections.

## Jewelry and hair accessories

Lock pair count, stones, chain links, clasps, connecting rings, ornament count, symmetry/asymmetry, and finish. Prevent fused chains, broken connections, duplicates, missing mates, and scale drift. Use broad side/back highlights for metal edge definition; avoid blown white highlights.

## Books and printed goods

Treat supplied artwork as immutable. Do not redraw exact covers when compositing is possible. Generate the surface, stand, shadow, or scene, then place the original cover through deterministic compositing. Reserve exact names, dimensions, arrows, and long copy for post-production.

## Beauty and packaged goods

Lock package count, bottle silhouette, cap/pump, label position, color, and size relationships. Arrange without overlap when label readability matters. Use clean gradients and controlled specular highlights; avoid invented labels or claims.

## Apparel

Lock neckline, sleeve length, waistline, hem length, closure, paneling, trim, pattern distribution, fabric behavior, accessories, and layer order. Choose crop based on the selling feature and keep hands from covering it. If the face is outside the crop, omit unnecessary facial specifications. Dark clothing needs separation from the background; textured fabric benefits from directional side light.

## Costume and cosplay

In addition to apparel locks, define whether wig, shoes, props, armor, and makeup belong to the product truth. Do not invent weapons, emblems, jewelry, or character accessories. Keep left/right placement and layer order correct. Separate catalog/editorial scenes from cinematic convention scenes; do not mix their lighting instructions.

## Multi-product and variants

List the exact count and identity of every item. Require one appearance per variant, consistent scale, no overlaps where comparison matters, and a clear ordering rule. For more than four visually complex products, prefer deterministic layout/compositing.
